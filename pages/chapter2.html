2 - Once upon a dream

❯

Uchem suddenly drops to her knees, clasping her hands tightly.

“Please forgive my mother!” she rasps out, chin lifted, and eyes closed.

Oh, Uchem.

Trying hard to mask my smile as I push my bedroom door closed, I turn back to her.

“Nah, it's not a big deal.”

Uchem’s eyes follow me as I land on my bed with a grunt, the foam compressing on impact and reviving seconds after.

“You’re not mad?” Uchem unfolds herself from her kneeling position and settles on the floor, resting her hands on the bed.

“Nope,” I say, staring right at her.

Don’t blink, don’t blink…

“Nope?” Her lips curve into a knowing smile.

“Fine,” I whisper, “maybe it stings just a bit.”

“I’m really sorry.” Uchem suddenly looks down at her fingers, her brows creasing slightly.

“When’s graduation?” I blurt out. “Any anko to wear?”

“You’ll really come to my graduation?” She sits up immediately.

“What kind of question is that?”

Uchem springs up from the floor, launching herself at me.

“I love you so much!” She tackles me into the mattress with a crushing hug.

“Yeah, yeah. I know. Stop. DON’T KISS ME!”

With an exasperated sigh, Uchem falls back on the bed, reaching for my palm and intertwining our fingers.

“How are you?” She breathes out.

I don’t even know.

I lift our intertwined hands, admiring her long and slim fingers next to my chubby, short ones.

“Behind,” I mutter.

Uchem turns sharply to me. “Oh, come on. Please don’t take what my mum said to heart.”

“I’m kidding.”

“Besides, she didn’t even acknowledge that you are studying a much better course than I did.” She shrugs.

“Better, uhn?”

What pretty nails she's got.

“Of course! You’d most probably earn a thousand times what I would ever earn. That’s even if I get a job.”

“Theatre Arts is not bad.” Finally dropping our hands, I turn towards her. “At least AI won’t replace you.”

“AI?” She turns fully to me. “You really think AI can actually have sense like you?”

I laugh out loud.

“You’d be the one controlling it instead.”

I hum in reply.

Uchem suddenly goes silent, staring at me.

“What?”

“It’s just too bad you stopped writing stories.”

Oh, come on.

“Remember when we dreamed together that I’d direct the movies of your stories? Now that you are not writing anymore, tell me how I would get a job.”

“Stop exaggerating, Uchem.”

“You fired me before I even graduated.”

“Stop messing around; you could end up working for big-time Nollywood people. Uche Montana is one; look, you almost have the same name.”

“I want you!”

“I’m giving you good recommendations!”

Uche huffs. “Is there really no way to write even if you are studying CS? You wrote really good stuff.”

I slip my hands out of hers and edge towards the end of the bed. “Nah-uh.”

“Remember Twinkle? Gianna? What was that twin story again? Iris and Sari!”

“Ugh, Uchem!”

“You didn’t even finish Three for Revenge; you stopped when we finished Secondary School…”

“Exactly!” I snap. “I’m all grown up now, and I need money! Not cringy stories about losing memories from falling off the cliff and going missing for years!”

Uchem’s eyes light up as she gasps, “You still remember Twinkle!”

I narrow my eyes at her. “Stop.”

She rolls her eyes. “Whatever.”

“I'm going to the toilet!” Pushing the bathroom door in, I turn sharply to Uchem, who is already up from the bed.

“Don’t follow me!”

“Do you have any boyfriends?” Uchem peeks through the door.

Oh, Uchem.

“Close it!” I hiss, flicking my hand toward the door.

“Answer me first.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“I’m using the toilet! Uchem!”

“So?”

“God, you’re so annoying!”

“Stop pretending you don’t enjoy it!” She slams the door shut.

Whatever.
