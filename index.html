Where This Goes S1

1 - I wish I would

❯

It feels like I’m on a pitch-black road — and Taylor Swift's shining ever so bright at the end of it.

‘I wish you would’ currently blasts in my ears, but it’s already blending with the background.

Her songs, her stories are just - - just so real.

If I told a story, sharing how lost I feel about my life, would others be able to relate?

I wish I could ask her how she knew what she wanted when she was young, how she kept believing in that dream, and worked on it to become something she’ll be proud of forever.

I scratch my head, shifting against the uncomfortable bed fabric as tears stream down my face.

I wonder if abandoning this gift would bring me peace. Maybe this path I am currently walking on will block out the voices in my—

Thud… Thud… Thud…

My eyes open.

“Iyanu!”

I sit up fast, blinking repeatedly to get my head straight.

The song.

I pull off my earpiece, its plastic scraping against my ear. I wince, ignoring the lingering throb as I scramble out of my bed to look for my flip-flops that have suddenly disappeared from their usual spot.

Oh, come on!

My bare feet hit the cold tile floor, and I reach out to turn the doorknob, pulling the door in.

Mum’s eyes coldly watch me straighten, rolling my shoulders back as I let out a shaky breath.

“Ma?” I force a smile.

Mum tilts her head, her eyes dropping from my face to my feet, and back to my face again.

“What's that? Were you crying?”

Shit.

My hands fly up to my face, scrubbing at my eyes and cheeks frantically.

Why the hell did I forget to clean it off earlier? She’s not going to let me off the hook!

I put on my best smile as I drop my hands from my face, but her gaze only sharpens.

“It’s nothing, I swear.”

“You better tell me the truth—”

“Ọyi m!” Our heads snap towards the living room, and I exhale out loud, much to Mum’s disappointment.

She passes me one last glare, which very well means the conversation is not over. “Iya Uchem is here, with Uchem.” She says, heading towards the living room.

Damn, that was close.

I close the door, straightening my shirt and my hair as I walk down the hallway to the living room.

Uchem's mum sits across from mine, laughing heartily before taking a sip of water, while Uchem lies slumped on the couch, legs shaking in absolute boredom.

She probably wishes she were somewhere else right now or is impatiently waiting to reunite with her high-school best friend.

“Iyanu!” She’s up from the chair and jumps on me before I can process it.

“Hey.” I giggle, wrapping my arms around her.

“It’s been a long time!” She finally pulls away.

“Yeah.” I take a step back, turning to her mum.

“Good evening, ma.” My knees dip a little to greet her respectfully.

“Asa nwa ” Uchem’s mum replies. “You did not even visit my daughter; she got back from her university just last week.”

Her tone’s a bit sharper, or was I imagining it?

“I was busy, ma.”

“Hmm, you are in 200 level abi?”

“300 level now,” Mum replies almost instantly.

“Ah, you still have a long way to go.” Her lips curved slightly. “Uchem will soon start NYSC, and you both graduated high school together.”

Uchem mouths a sorry the moment our eyes meet.

“God’s time is the best.” My attention shifts to Mum, her jaw hardening as she giggles.

“Of course, we have to thank God that she finally got her life moving after so many years at home.”

Wow.
